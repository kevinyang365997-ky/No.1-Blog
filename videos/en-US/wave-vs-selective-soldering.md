# Video Overview

Wave and selective soldering can both join THT components, but they process the PCB differently. The cost difference involves not only the machine, but also control accuracy, programming flexibility, tooling and product mix.

## Wave Soldering Characteristics

- A continuous solder wave contacts a broad area on the underside of the PCB.
- It suits stable, high-volume products with many solder joints.
- Board throughput can be high, but non-solder areas may require masking or dedicated tooling.

## Selective Soldering Characteristics

- A programmed nozzle solders individual points or defined areas.
- Position, contact time, solder flow and movement path can be controlled precisely.
- It better supports dense PCBs, mixed assembly, varied products and protected areas.

## Why the Cost Structure Is Different

1. Selective soldering requires more precise motion and programming systems.
2. Individual joints need controlled positioning and process stability.
3. Equipment often combines fluxing, preheating, soldering and monitoring modules.
4. Higher flexibility can reduce dedicated tooling, but initial investment is usually higher.
5. The final economics depend on volume, changeovers, quality risk and labor cost.

## Practical View

The more expensive machine is not automatically the best choice, and a lower purchase price does not always mean a lower total cost. The process should match PCB design, product mix, volume and quality requirements.
